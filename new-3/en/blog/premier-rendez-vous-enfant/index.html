    Your child’s first appointment at the dentist

    How a first check-up goes, what to say at home before coming, and why the appointment moves at the child’s pace.

    Book an appointmentCall the practice

    A child’s first appointment is above all about getting to know each other. The child discovers the chair, the light, the mirror, the person who is going to count their teeth. The dentist looks at the teeth, the gums and the way the teeth are coming through. Then the dentist talks with you about habits at home. If all is well, the appointment stops there. That is already a good result, because the child leaves with a neutral or pleasant memory.

    What to say before coming

    Simple, neutral words are enough. You can say the dentist is going to count their teeth and check they are nice and clean. Avoid promising that nothing will happen, and avoid telling your own experiences. Also avoid words that reassure by naming a fear, such as “it won’t hurt”. The child had not thought about pain before the word arrived.

    If treatment is needed

    The dentist explains what was seen, what the treatment aims at and how it may go, in words suited to the age. The pace takes the child into account. When several steps are planned, they are spread out so that each appointment stays short. The plan is presented to the parents before deciding what comes next, as for an adult.

    Preventing cavities at home

    Prevention relies on brushing, diet and check-ups adapted to the child’s risk. The practice shows the areas to watch and adapts the advice to the child’s age and independence. It also answers your questions about the daily routine. The recommendations change as the child grows, and no single rule suits every family. That is why they are given at the appointment.

    Booking the appointment

    When booking, give the child’s age and the reason for the appointment, a first check-up, a wobbly tooth, a mark you noticed. The team plans the right amount of time.
