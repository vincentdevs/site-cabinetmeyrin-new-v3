    Whitening, what changes and what stays

    Whitening lightens natural teeth, not crowns or fillings. What to check beforehand, what to expect, and why the assessment comes first.

    Book an appointmentCall the practice

    Whitening acts on the shade of natural teeth. It does not lighten a crown, a veneer or a composite filling, which keep their original colour. That is the first thing to know. A restored front tooth can stand out darker after a successful whitening of its neighbours. The preliminary assessment is precisely there to spot that kind of situation before starting.

    What the assessment checks

    Before any whitening, the dentist checks for the absence of cavities and the state of the gums. The dentist also looks for restorations on the visible teeth. An untreated cavity or a receding gum lets the product reach sensitive areas. Scaling often comes before whitening. A deposit of tartar or a surface stain comes off without any lightening product. The result is then judged on clean teeth.

    What to expect

    The result depends on the starting shade, the cause of the discolouration and how your teeth respond. Staining linked to coffee, tea or tobacco generally responds well. A grey shade from birth or an old discolouration within the thickness of the tooth responds less. The practitioner tells you what you can reasonably expect in your case, and shows you your starting shade on a shade guide.

    Sensitivity during treatment

    Sensitivity to cold during the treatment days is common and disappears once it stops. It is managed by spacing out the sessions or adjusting the concentration of the product. Mention it, the protocol adapts.

    After whitening

    The shade obtained changes over time, depending on habits. Limiting staining drinks in the first days and keeping a regular hygiene routine prolongs the result. A touch-up can be considered later, always after a check-up.
