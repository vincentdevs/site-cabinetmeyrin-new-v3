    Oral surgery and stomatology in Meyrin

    Dr Edouard Di Donna, a Swiss federal specialist in oral surgery, dedicates his practice exclusively to oral surgery and stomatology. He assesses and treats conditions affecting the teeth, oral mucosa, jaws and associated tissues. Care is proposed after a thorough clinical assessment, in coordination with the other practitioners at the practice and referring doctors.

    Book an appointmentCall the practice

    Areas of care

    The choice of treatment depends on the examination and the indications in each individual case. The consultation also considers alternatives before a procedure is planned.

    Dental surgery and complex extractions

    Simple and complex tooth extractions, including impacted or partially impacted wisdom teeth. Orthodontic surgery includes cerclage, bone anchorage, corticotomies and frenectomies. Apical surgery and apicectomies are also provided.

    Oral implantology and bone reconstruction

    Planning and placement of dental implants, including extraction and immediate implant placement when conditions allow. Procedures include surgical treatment of peri-implantitis, horizontal or vertical bone grafting and elevation of the sinus floor (sinus lift).

    Stomatology and oral medicine

    Diagnosis and treatment of changes and lesions of the oral mucosa: diagnostic biopsies, surgical excision of selected lesions and monitoring of chronic mucosal conditions.

    Soft tissue and preprosthetic surgery

    Soft tissue grafts and procedures to prepare tissues for functional or prosthetic rehabilitation, including removal of exostoses and vestibuloplasty.

    Jaw cysts and tumoral pathologies

    Diagnosis and surgical treatment of jaw cysts and tumoral bone pathologies, according to the clinical assessment.

    Salivary gland conditions

    Assessment and management of selected salivary gland conditions: salivary stones, mucoceles and lesions of the minor salivary glands.

    Temporomandibular joint (TMJ) dysfunction

    Diagnosis and management of temporomandibular joint disorders and associated orofacial pain.

    How is a procedure prepared?

    The practitioner details the planned step, the useful examinations, the instructions before the appointment and the usual aftermath. They take into account the medicines, history and allergies reported by the patient.

    The course and the risks depend on the procedure. They are therefore explained for your situation, with the available alternatives and the reasons for the proposed choice.

    What happens after the procedure?

    Written or spoken instructions cover hygiene, food, taking the prescribed medicines and the signs that should lead you to call the practice. The check-up schedule depends on the procedure carried out and on healing.

    If pain or bleeding evolves in a way that worries you, contact the practice. In case of difficulty breathing or a life-threatening situation, call 144.

    Does a procedure need assessing?

    The consultation confirms the indication and explains the options.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is an extraction always necessary when a tooth hurts?

    No. Pain can have several causes. The examination determines whether the tooth can be treated or whether an extraction should be discussed.

    Is an X-ray needed?

    An image is suggested when it provides information needed for the decision or to prepare the procedure.

    How much time should I allow?

    The length of the procedure and of healing varies with the step and the situation. The practitioner gives you an estimate after the assessment.

    Do you have a question about this treatment?

    The appointment is there to examine your situation and explain what is possible. You decide afterwards.
