    Cavities in Meyrin, diagnosis and treatment

    The dentist examines the tooth, assesses how far the decay has spread and removes the affected tissue when necessary. The tooth is then restored if it can be kept. If the decay is close to or inside the pulp, a root canal treatment may be discussed.

    Book an appointmentCall the practice

    Does a cavity always cause symptoms?

    No. A cavity may not hurt at first. As it progresses, sensitivity to cold, heat or sugar can appear. Discomfort when chewing, a hole or pain are other possible signs. These signs can also have other causes.

    Pain that fades does not prove the problem is solved. The diagnosis depends on examining the tooth.

    How does the dentist confirm a cavity?

    The dentist asks what you feel, examines the tooth and carries out the necessary checks. An X-ray may be suggested if it can show a hidden area between teeth or under an old repair.

    They also look at why the cavity may have appeared and whether it is approaching the living part inside the tooth.

    What treatment is proposed?

    When the tooth can be restored, the affected tissue is removed and the shape rebuilt with a suitable material. The remaining volume, the position and the forces at play guide the type of restoration.

    If the decay has reached the pulp, a root canal treatment may be needed. A very weakened tooth may then need wider protection, such as a crown, depending on the examination.

    Is a tooth sensitive or painful?

    The appointment is there to find the cause and the appropriate treatment.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can a cavity be stopped without treatment?

    When it is only just starting, an area can sometimes be monitored with preventive measures. Once a hole has formed, the tooth generally needs to be treated.

    Why can a cavity come back?

    A new cavity can form near an old repair if the causes that favoured it are still present. Follow-up therefore also covers hygiene and habits.

    What should I do in case of severe pain?

    Call the practice to have the tooth checked. If swelling makes breathing difficult or the situation is life-threatening, call 144.

    Do you have a question about this treatment?

    The appointment is there to examine your situation and explain what is possible. You decide afterwards.
